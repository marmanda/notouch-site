For therapists and researchers

This page is dedicated to therapists and researchers who are interested in using no-touch instruments for rehabilitation and therapy. The tool in this page are designed to support the development of motor skills, cognitive abilities, and emotional well-being in individuals with various needs. The instruments are designed to be adaptable and to accomodate various degrees of difficulty in the same movement. Moreover they allow the log for every patient and the recording of the data of the sessions, which can be used for research purposes.

Tool Description Features

Fingernote_v3.16

 This interactive tool aims to enhance fine motor coordination and dexterity of both hands. A note from the C major scale is assigned to each finger and is activated when the user pinches that finger against the thumb. The volume of the sound is dynamically adjusted according to the distance of the hands from the screen, providing an engaging multimodal experience that combines motor training and musical interaction. Visual feedback is provided through note colors/finger numbers on the tip of the fingers and pinch activation.
    - Full scale (from C4 to C5, starting from the left little finger and ending with the right little finger)
  • - Half scale (the first four notes for both hands)
  • - Division of the space into two different octaves. Upper space, higher octave
  • - Interactive score with reset command (R)
  • - Four levels of pinch sensitivity
  • - Four levels of finger extension
  • - Patient logging
  • - Data recording

Handwave Synth v.2.17

 Handwave Synth uses the movement of the index finger to generate a continuous sound whose pitch and volume change dynamically according to the finger's position in space. Moving the finger higher increases the pitch, while moving it to the left decreases the volume. A colored circle, accompanied by a motion trail, provides visual feedback by tracking the finger's movement. In tonal mode, the circle adopts the color associated with the currently played note, following a predefined note-color mapping. Horizontal lines help to user in identifying the note they ae playing
  • Show or hide the camera image
  • Show or hide the visual trails
  • Attract or not pitch toward musical notes
  • Control the amount of note attraction
  • Constrain notes a musical scale
  • Select three different scales
  • Select the playable pitch range
  • Select the volume range
  • Patient logging
  • Open and close experimental sessions

Skies

 Transforms movement into wind. Through touchless interaction, performers shape invisible air currents that become sound, light, and motion. Horizontal hand movement controls the brightness of the sound, while vertical movement adjusts resonance, allowing users to sculpt the character and intensity of the airflow.
  • Material exploration
  • Movement & Transformation
